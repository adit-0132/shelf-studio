# Category playbooks

Structure guidance for common listing shapes. These are editorial recommendations, not material-performance facts, and they do not supply missing specifications. Use the closest section for structure only; facts always come from the seller's inputs and the original photos. A knowledge pack (see README.md) can replace this file with researched playbooks for a specific category.

## Multi-piece sets and collections

State the exact count and enumerate components where known. Translate the set into its practical arrangement: pieces of different sizes for different roles, matching pairs, or an orderly group. Do not call it a complete set unless its inclusions support that description.

For ten or more pieces, keep every known dimension and individual weight in a structured specification table. Use prose to explain the mix of forms, not to duplicate every row. Do not imply the weight is shipping weight; do not automatically total weights when rows may be a partial sample. If the seller supplies only three measurements for ten pieces, those three are measured examples, not evidence that all ten share the values.

Use a collective noun such as set, pair or collection only when it identifies the product. One precise regional noun plus its plain-English name is enough. Do not produce a synonym list in the title.

## Gift sets and festive collections

Describe what makes the gift tangible: the coordinated pieces, finish, useful format and how they look together. Housewarming, wedding or festive gifting may be positioned as suitable occasions when consistent with the item and seller brief. Do not promise a gift box, ribbon, personalisation or ready-to-gift packaging unless provided.

A premium tone should be welcoming, not a caricature of royalty. Use an occasion-led paragraph sparingly. If “Diwali” is requested, include it naturally in a relevant bullet or description; keep the actual product identity central. A ritual product is not automatically food-safe tableware.

## New or unfamiliar products

Begin from the product's plain-English identity and ask what the user-provided evidence establishes. Use the closest playbook only for structure. Do not squeeze every item into a gifting or occasion story. If the product belongs to a category these playbooks don't cover, produce grounded copy using the core style guide and record a knowledge gap for future research. No performance fact may be invented to make an existing template fit.
