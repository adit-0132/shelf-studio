# Product listing writing system

A brand- and category-neutral editorial system for marketplace listing copy. Marketplace field limits and their sources are in marketplace-rules.md; structure for common listing shapes is in category-playbooks.md. This is editorial guidance, not a claim that any one writing pattern improves conversion.

## Voice

Write for a buyer who wants to understand exactly what they are getting: the product's identity, material, design and the role it plays. If the seller positions the brand as premium, convey that through observation and useful specificity rather than superlatives. A brand name does not establish handmade production, regional origin, artisan relationships, age, sustainability or any certification.

The default voice is composed, warm, design-aware and precise. Use natural Indian English, with British spellings such as colour and organisation, unless the seller's comments ask for something else. Address the product and its role rather than repeatedly addressing the reader as a discerning customer. Prefer complete sentences with an unhurried cadence. One evocative phrase should be followed by something the buyer can see or verify.

Good direction: describe the contrast between a polished surface and its dark floral detailing, explain how differently sized pieces relate to each other, then state exactly what is included. Weak direction: call a product luxurious, exquisite, royal and timeless in the same sentence without adding a concrete detail.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Seller-entered brand, product identity, material, number of pieces, named measurements and weights.
2. Extra specifications and restrictions explicitly supplied in Additional comments.
3. Clear visual observations in original photos: visible shape, pattern, finish appearance, components and colour.
4. Knowledge-base recommendations about language and structure.
5. Seller-approved past copy, for style only.

Never infer from a photograph: material grade or purity, plating, the identity of a stone, metallurgy, food-contact or skin-contact suitability, seal performance, capacity, manufacturing method, care compatibility, warranty or country of origin. Never borrow these facts from another brand's listing. If a comment explicitly corrects a structured field, surface the conflict for the seller rather than quietly advertising one of two conflicting values. Keep uncertainties in internal review notes, not in customer-facing copy. Comments may contain writing requests, additional product facts, or explicit image directions; preserve those distinct meanings.

## Title architecture

Create two versions, with different destinations:

- Detailed catalogue title: brand (only if supplied) + exact product identity + confirmed material + defining visible design/finish + set count + useful size or purpose. Aim for 130–190 characters when the facts justify it; the app permits at most 220. This is an editorial range for a product catalogue, not an Amazon field limit. Do not add weak adjectives simply to reach the range.
- Amazon title: the brand (only if supplied), the clearest category phrase and one or two decisive facts within 75 characters. Move supplementary details to the separate 125-character Item Highlights field. Keep both readable on their own.

Put the plain noun a shopper would search for early, naming the product type as it is commonly sold. Use one useful Indian synonym when it genuinely names the item, not a chain of aliases. Do not state a capacity, size or weight unless supplied. Length/breadth is not automatically diameter. A decorative band is not automatically hand engraving. Never invent a brand when none is supplied.

Constructed example, not a seller SKU: given a confirmed three-piece stainless steel canister set with visible floral patterns and no brand supplied, a short title could be “Stainless Steel Floral Canister Set, 3 Pieces”. The highlights can cover the visible contrast and intended placement. That example does not establish airtightness, gold plating, engraving or food storage suitability.

## Five feature bullets

Use five distinct ideas. Give each a short sentence-case lead-in, then the feature and its practical or visual value. Aim for roughly 170–300 characters per bullet when useful; never exceed the app's 500-character ceiling. These length targets are our editing guidance, not universal rules across every category.

1. Product identity and distinguishing design: what it is, confirmed material and the defining visible feature.
2. A second specific detail: form, finish contrast, inclusion or mechanism that is actually documented.
3. Set composition and scale: piece count, named sizes, supplied capacities or exact L × B × H, with units.
4. Appropriate use and occasion: explain where the product belongs (at home, on a table or shelf, worn, displayed, gifted or used in ritual) without inventing functional suitability.
5. A remaining useful fact: a confirmed care instruction, restriction, supplied packaging detail, or another non-repetitive inclusion/design point. Never manufacture a warranty or care claim to fill this slot.

Do not repeat the entire title five times. Do not use all-uppercase paragraphs, decorative emojis, promises of perfection or a fake urgency. If only a few facts exist, write shorter bullets. A sensible use suggestion is not a tested performance claim.

## Two descriptions

The Amazon description is the marketplace copy field. Aim to make it substantial within 2,000 characters, typically 1,300–1,900 when enough facts are available. State identity, design, the set, practical details and confirmed restrictions in connected paragraphs. The limit is the app's conservative default informed by Amazon seller guidance; the account's current category schema remains authoritative.

The long description is an expanded catalogue/store draft, normally 350–650 words in four to seven paragraphs. It is intentionally separate from the Amazon description field. More words must add understanding, not more adjectives. A complex ten-piece set can need additional factual space; a simple item with sparse inputs should not be padded to a rigid target.

Suggested paragraph sequence:

1. Establish the object and its atmosphere. Anchor the opening in a specific visible or provided detail.
2. Explore material and appearance. Describe how the surface, motif, shape and proportions relate. Avoid turning a visible effect into a manufacturing claim.
3. Explain the set, if there is one. Name the pieces and explain their different sizes or roles, using exact facts. A separate specifications table preserves every measurement.
4. Place it in a believable setting or occasion that suits the product, such as a dining table, shelf, dressing table, workspace, celebration or housewarming gift. Never promise gift packaging unless supplied.
5. Address practical details and confirmed care or restrictions. If these are unknown, do not invent them. Close on the product's concrete contribution, not a call to buy now.

Vary openings between products. Do not repeatedly begin with “Elevate your…”, “Introducing…”, “Discover the perfect blend…” or “Crafted for the discerning…”. Do not fabricate a brand origin story to make the long description longer. Do not copy or lightly paraphrase marketplace descriptions.

## Rich language with factual boundaries

Useful when visually supported: polished, reflective, matte, rounded, cylindrical, floral, geometric, contrasting, coordinated, understated, decorative, silver-toned, gold-toned, warm-toned, patterned, textured, tapered, lidded, clear.

Use only when supplied or adequately documented for this exact item: hand-hammered, hand-engraved, hand-painted, handmade, artisan-made, solid brass, pure copper, 925 sterling silver, gold-plated, hallmarked, genuine leather, 100% cotton, 304 grade, 18/8, food-grade, airtight, leak-proof, dishwasher-safe, waterproof, hypoallergenic, nickel-free, tarnish-resistant, rust-proof, scratch-resistant, sustainable, ethically sourced, gift-boxed, lifetime warranty.

Avoid: best, finest, ultimate, world-class, flawless, perfect for everyone, unbreakable, guaranteed to last generations, detoxifying, immunity-boosting, antibacterial health promises, chemical-free and toxin-free. “Premium” can position the brand, but should not become a substitute for material and design detail.

A gold-coloured motif is a gold-toned motif, not gold plating. A visible hammered texture is a hammered-look texture unless the process is confirmed. A lid is a lid, not proof of an airtight seal. A metal's appearance does not identify its grade or purity, and a stone's colour does not identify the stone. Brass and bronze are not interchangeable labels. External dimensions do not establish usable capacity.

## Handling comments

- “Describe this as a Diwali gift, no royal language” changes emphasis and vocabulary.
- “Hand wash only; not for direct flame” adds product restrictions which must be preserved.
- “Include 350 ml capacity” adds a capacity claim only if supplied as a fact, not as a guess or question.
- “Do not mention weight in the title” changes placement; the given weight remains in specifications.
- “Use warm light in the images” affects imagery only.
- “Maybe 304 steel?” is unresolved and must not become a 304-grade claim.
- A writing example is a style reference, not product evidence. Never copy its measurements, features, origin story or wording into the next item.

## Consistency as the catalogue grows

Every entry retains its input facts, comments, generated text, five images and the exact writing-guide snapshot used. New entries use the current guide, while old entries remain reproducible. Only listings deliberately marked by the seller as a writing example are retrieved as style references. Choose at most three examples with related product/material words; no example may override the new facts.

Refresh platform rules when they change. Do not claim that the knowledge base continuously crawls marketplaces: research updates are deliberate, versioned edits. The local catalogue grows automatically; the external research does not refresh itself.

## Final editing pass

Check identity, exact count, included components, all dimensions and weights, material wording, tone instructions, care restrictions, distinct bullets, title limits and description limits. Remove unsupported claims and repeated keywords. Confirm that every public sentence could be explained by an input fact, a visible feature, or a clearly framed use suggestion. Mark uncertain critical facts for seller review without leaking internal commentary into export copy.
